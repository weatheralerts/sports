Why sports venues need personal thresholds

A general forecast says it might rain. A club needs to know whether rain will be heavy enough to waterlog greens, whether gusts will make spraying impossible, whether freezing conditions will leave a pitch unplayable on Saturday, and above all whether lightning is likely while people are out on an open course. Those decisions depend on specific numbers.

Weather Alerts is an Android app built around those specific numbers. The Weather Alerts website shows how you can set custom thresholds for precipitation intensity, temperature, wind speed and gusts, UV index, humidity, visibility and more, then receive notifications when they are met.

Lightning and storm awareness

Open courses and pitches are exposed places in a thunderstorm. Weather description alerts can flag thunderstorms and lightning in the forecast, and the radar includes a lightning tracker and storm radar to follow activity as it develops. Official severe thunderstorm warnings appear where supported national meteorological services issue them, with agency attribution.

These tools help you prepare, but they are not a lightning detection system and do not replace your club's lightning policy, on-site detectors or the judgement of officials. When thunder is heard, follow your safety procedures.

How a club might set it up

  1. Save the venue and practice areas. Add the course, courts, main pitch and training ground as separate locations if they are spread out.
  2. Create rules for play and maintenance. For example: heavy rain, thunderstorms, gusts above the spraying limit, freezing conditions overnight, heat index above your junior-session limit.
  3. Choose lead times. Fixture decisions might use several days of notice; daily play might use same-day alerts.

Fixture decisions with lead time

Calling off a fixture is easier for everyone when it happens early. Because alerts can arrive up to seven days before the expected weather, club secretaries can warn opponents, referees and caterers about a likely postponement well ahead, then confirm closer to the day using the daily and hourly forecasts. The weather front view helps judge whether a wet spell will clear in time for a weekend match.

Clubs that share one set of saved venues and thresholds across several staff phones keep everybody working from the same information.

Heat and sun for players

Long rounds and summer tournaments can mean hours in the sun. Heat index and temperature spike alerts help organisers plan water points, shade and schedule changes, while UV index alerts remind players about sun protection. Hourly forecasts show temperature, feels-like, precipitation probability, wind and humidity for the day of play.

A match week in practice

Tuesday: a heavy-rain alert for Friday night prompts the groundskeeper to plan covers for the square and to delay line marking. Wednesday: a gust alert for Thursday rules out spraying, so it moves to Wednesday afternoon. Friday: a freezing-conditions alert for early Saturday leads to a pitch inspection being scheduled before players travel. Saturday: thunderstorms appear in the forecast description for late afternoon, so the club briefs officials on the lightning policy and keeps an eye on the lightning tracker.

Each step is a decision your team already makes; alerts simply bring the information forward so it can be made calmly.

For individual players too

You do not need to run a club to benefit. A golfer can set a calm, dry rule for the weekend; a tennis player can watch rain stop alerts for the local courts; a parent can follow heat and lightning alerts for a child's tournament. Save the venues you use most and let the app watch them.

Away fixtures and tournaments

Travelling teams can save the away venue, use route weather to track rain, snow, wind, storms, visibility and temperature on the drive, and rely on live location weather once they arrive.

Notifications for busy clubs

Custom sounds, Quiet Hours and Active Hours let staff receive routine alerts during working hours while critical warnings can still come through at any time. Widgets put current conditions and active alerts on the home screen of the greenkeeper's or secretary's phone.

Data and Premium

Course, court and pitch forecasts are provided through Apple Weather (WeatherKit), which brings together data from national and international meteorological agencies. Using a single, consistent source makes it easier to compare conditions across a club's different venues over the season.

Weather Alerts is available on Google Play for Android. Premium includes push notifications for custom alerts, more alerts and locations, extended forecasts and an ad-free experience, with a seven-day free trial billed by Google. Read more about custom rain and wind alerts on the official site.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get warned about lightning before a round of golf?

Weather description alerts can flag thunderstorms and lightning in the forecast, and the lightning tracker shows activity on the radar.

Is Weather Alerts a lightning detection system?

No. It uses forecast and radar data and does not replace on-site detection or your club's lightning policy.

Can our groundskeeper set a wind limit for spraying?

Yes. Set wind speed and gust thresholds for the venue.

Will I know if courts will be wet tomorrow?

Rain alerts can notify you in advance when precipitation is expected to start or stop.

Can a club monitor several pitches?

Yes. Save each pitch or site separately with its own rules.

Is there a frost alert for greens?

A freezing-conditions or low-temperature alert for the course can remind staff to protect greens.

Can coaches get heat alerts for junior sessions?

Yes. Heat index and temperature thresholds can be set for the training ground.
